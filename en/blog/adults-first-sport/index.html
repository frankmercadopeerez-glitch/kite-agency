Starting kitesurfing as an adult without boardsport experience

Prepare an introduction around your current skills and learning pace rather than comparisons with experienced riders.

Short answer

Prepare an introduction around your current skills and learning pace rather than comparisons with experienced riders.

In this guide

  1. Describe experience instead of assuming ability
  2. Choose a small goal
  3. Adjust the organisation
  4. Finish with an informed decision

Describe experience instead of assuming ability

Explain your usual activities and how comfortable you feel in the sea. Never having surfed or skated does not predict a lesson’s outcome. The initial conversation helps select suitable tasks, breaks and explanations rather than assign talent or limitations by age.

Choose a small goal

Understanding a signal, identifying a component or repeating a controlled movement are useful early goals. A series of small, observable achievements provides a clearer foundation than trying to reproduce a jump seen on social media.

Adjust the organisation

Ask about individual attention, session length and spreading learning across several days. Mention needs that affect participation so the school can assess the format. If you need a health assessment, discuss it with an appropriate professional rather than relying on a commercial promise.

Finish with an informed decision

Request a summary of what you learned and what continuing will involve. You can then decide between a one-off experience and ongoing progression. The first session need not prove you will master the sport; it should help you understand how you learn and the support you need.
