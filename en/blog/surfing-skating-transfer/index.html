What may feel familiar

Board experience may help you recognise balance and movement sensations. It can also help you explain those feelings to an instructor. This familiarity is not kite control or knowledge of its systems, so it does not replace a kite-specific introduction.

A different source of pull

Kiting introduces an airborne component connected by lines. You must learn references and communication as well as board skills. Surfing waves or descending a slope confidently does not mean you can select, prepare or use this equipment without instruction.

How to describe yourself when booking

Explain your sport, frequency and water experience without automatically assigning yourself a kitesurf level. Ask for an assessment that uses your strengths and identifies what is new. Good adaptation avoids both unnecessary repetition and skipping important content.

Compare progress with your starting point

One task may feel natural while another needs patience. That difference does not necessarily mean the course is poorly matched. Record kite-specific skills and decide how to continue from those, rather than your level in another sport.
