Damaged kite: diagnosis, repair or replacement

Describe pressure loss, canopy tears and line damage, and understand the scope of a repair before using the equipment again.

Short answer

Describe pressure loss, canopy tears and line damage, and understand the scope of a repair before using the equipment again.

In this guide

  1. Start with the symptom
  2. Separate types of damage
  3. Lines and control systems
  4. Understand the handover

Start with the symptom

Provide brand, model, year, size, incident history and previous repairs. Add overall photographs and close-ups with scale. “Loses pressure after this interval” is more useful than assuming the bladder must be replaced. A technician needs to identify the failed component before choosing an intervention.

Separate types of damage

Pressure loss can involve different parts of the inflatable system; the visible valve is not automatically the source. Canopy tears, open seams and reinforcement damage also need different assessments. Avoid improvised glue or continued use that hides evidence or enlarges damage. Tape covering a tear does not demonstrate structural integrity.

Lines and control systems

Lines, connections and bar work as a set. Improvised knots or lookalike parts can change their behaviour. Remove equipment from use if you find damage, length differences or irregular release operation. The quote should identify replacement references and compatibility, not merely say “bar repaired”.

Understand the handover

Request diagnosis, scope, parts, cost, estimated timing and remaining limitations. Physical inspection and parts availability may affect the quote. Ask which checks were completed before return, and retain the repair history. The repair service page handles availability; this guide prepares you to understand the technical discussion.
