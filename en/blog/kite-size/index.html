Choosing kite size: wind, rider, board and model

Gather the right information before selecting a kite; understand why a universal wind-to-size chart is insufficient.

Kite Cartagena editorial team · Reviewed: 2026-09-27

Short answer

Gather the right information before selecting a kite; understand why a universal wind-to-size chart is insufficient.

In this guide

  1. Area is only one specification
  2. Gather the inputs
  3. Consider variation and purpose
  4. Ask a complete question

Area is only one specification

Two kites with the same area can have different operating ranges and behaviour. Read the guidance for the actual model and year, including the compatible bar. A single table cannot reliably convert wind speed into the right size for every rider and board.

Gather the inputs

Record approximate rider weight, current ability, board type and size, kite model and manufacturer range. Add observed wind, gusts and direction relative to the shore. Do not copy another rider’s size when you do not know their equipment or ability. An unknown model or condition needs clarification first.

Consider variation and purpose

Identical average wind readings can hide different gust patterns. A measurement away from the beach may not describe the practice area. Do not treat a manufacturer’s upper range as a beginner training target. A choice made for a twin-tip also needs reassessment before a foil session or a different day.

Ask a complete question

Instead of “Should I bring my twelve?”, provide weight, board, model, year and current skill level, with the forecast as context. Confirm the choice locally. For rental, ask what happens if suitable compatible equipment is unavailable. A useful response may be a different set or no session, rather than a size number.
