What to do when your kitesurf progress feels stuck

Turn a repeated difficulty into an observable question to make better use of a refresher lesson.

Read the guide

Kite Cartagena editorial team · Reviewed: 2026-09-27

Short answer

Turn a repeated difficulty into an observable question to make better use of a refresher lesson.

In this guide

  1. Define the recurring problem
  2. Separate outcome from cause
  3. Change one thing at a time
  4. Finish with a decision

Define the recurring problem

“I am not progressing” combines different problems. Identify the stage that fails: preparation, start, riding or finish. Note whether it affects both directions and starts immediately or after a while. This precision makes an in-lesson assessment easier.

Separate outcome from cause

Falling is an outcome, not an explanation. Avoid deciding alone that you need another board or a larger kite. An instructor can assess coordination, understanding and suitability of conditions before attributing the problem to equipment.

Change one thing at a time

Agree on one priority for a short practice block and a way to review the outcome. Constantly changing the instruction makes patterns difficult to identify. Pausing to explain your understanding can help more than accumulating repetitions without a clear question.

Finish with a decision

Request a specific recommendation: continue the exercise, revisit a prerequisite or schedule a different refresher. Record the reason. Progress also means understanding a limitation and what to work on, even when no new manoeuvre appears that afternoon.
