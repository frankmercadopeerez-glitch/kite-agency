Using video analysis in a kitesurf lesson

Choose useful footage, a specific question and clear privacy arrangements when reviewing technique.

Read the guide

Kite Cartagena editorial team · Reviewed: 2026-09-27

Short answer

Choose useful footage, a specific question and clear privacy arrangements when reviewing technique.

In this guide

  1. Start with one question
  2. What needs to be visible
  3. Review a few sequences
  4. Delivery and image use

Start with one question

Video helps most when it answers something specific: why a start fails, when you lose direction or what differs between two attempts. Recording an entire afternoon without a goal creates much footage and little actionable information. Agree first on the exercise being observed.

What needs to be visible

Ask your instructor which framing supports that objective. A tight shot may hide the kite or trajectory; a distant shot may obscure posture. The person filming must stay outside the manoeuvring area and should not make you approach them for a better image.

Review a few sequences

Select one representative attempt and a contrasting one. Describe what you felt before reviewing the footage with your instructor. Identify one priority correction rather than an endless list of faults. Repeat the exercise only within the agreed teaching plan.

Delivery and image use

Distinguish technical analysis from a commercial photography session. Confirm delivery format, timing and publication permission for identifiable people. Receiving a video does not mean you consented to promotional use or that a technical assessment is automatically included.
